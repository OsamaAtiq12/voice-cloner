The big picture

Voice Cloner listens to a short recording of someone speaking, learns how their voice sounds, then reads new words in that same voice.

  1. Give it a voice sample — a short clip of the person talking.
  2. Type what you want said — any sentence or paragraph.
  3. Press Generate — the tool creates a new audio file you can play and download.

Everything runs on your computer. Your audio is not uploaded to the internet.

Quick start (the easy path)

  1. Open the Studio page.
  2. In Text to speak, type the words you want in the cloned voice.
  3. In Reference audio, drop a clear clip of the voice (about 3–10 seconds).
  4. Leave the other settings alone for now.
  5. Click Generate speech and wait a few seconds.
  6. Press play in the Output box, or download the WAV file.

That is all most people need. The rest of this page explains every option if you want more control.

What you fill in

Text to speak

The script the cloned voice will say. Type or paste anything — a sentence, a paragraph, or a longer script.

Language

Which language the text is in. Auto-detect is usually perfect — the tool figures it out. Only change this if the result sounds wrong for that language.

Reference audio

A short recording of the voice you want to copy. Think of it as a “voice fingerprint.”

  • Best length: 3 to 10 seconds
  • Best quality: clear speech, little background noise, one person talking
  • Formats: WAV, MP3, FLAC, and similar audio files work

Longer clips are not always better — short and clear usually clones more accurately.

Output

Where your finished audio appears. After generation you can play it here and download a WAV file to use in videos, messages, or other apps.

Speaking controls

These change how the speech sounds. Defaults are already good for most people.

Speed

How fast the voice talks. 1.00× is normal. Higher = faster (and shorter). Lower = slower (and longer).

Fixed duration (seconds)

Force the whole clip to last a specific number of seconds. Leave this empty (Auto) unless you need exact timing — for example, to fit a video slot. If you set a duration, it overrides Speed.

Quality steps

How carefully the AI builds the voice. More steps = better quality, but slower.

  • 16 — faster, good for quick drafts
  • 32 — balanced (recommended)
  • 48–64 — highest quality, takes longer

Cleaner speech (denoise)

When checked, the tool aims for cleaner, clearer speech. Keep this on unless you are experimenting.

Normalize numbers to words

When checked, numbers are read as words. Example: 2345 becomes “two thousand three hundred forty-five” instead of “two three four five.” Turn this on if your script has lots of numbers, prices, or years.

Advanced settings

You can ignore this whole section and still get great results. These are fine-tuning knobs for people who want to experiment.

Guidance scale

How strongly the AI sticks to your instructions and reference voice. Default 2.0 is a solid middle ground. Too high can sound stiff; too low can sound looser or less accurate.

Time shift

A technical timing setting for how the AI builds sound over time. Default 0.10 is recommended. Change only if you are testing.

Position temperature

Controls how “adventurous” the AI is when choosing what to generate next in the audio pattern. 0 is very predictable. Higher values add more variety (and sometimes more randomness). Default is 5.0.

Class temperature

Similar idea, but for the tiny sound pieces the AI picks. Default 0.0 is the safest / most consistent choice.

Layer penalty

Helps the AI build the voice in a stable order, from big structure to fine detail. Default 5.0 works well for most voices.

Pad silence (seconds)

Adds a tiny bit of quiet at the start and end of the audio so it does not feel cut off. Default 0.1 seconds. Set to 0 for no padding.

Fade in/out (seconds)

Softly fades the volume up at the start and down at the end, so clicks are less likely. Default 0.1 seconds. Set to 0 to turn fades off.

Chunk duration & chunk threshold

For long scripts, the tool splits the job into smaller pieces so your computer does not run out of memory.

  • Chunk threshold (30s default) — if the speech would be longer than this, chunking turns on.
  • Chunk duration (15s default) — roughly how long each piece should be.

For short clips, you can leave these alone.

Preprocess reference audio

Cleans up the sample before cloning — for example, trimming long silences. Keep this on for everyday use.

Post-process output (trim silence)

Cleans the finished audio by removing long quiet gaps. Keep this on unless you set a fixed duration and need the length to match exactly (in that case, turn this off).

Tips for best results

  • Use a quiet room and a clear recording of one speaker.
  • Prefer 3–10 seconds of natural talking over a long, noisy clip.
  • If the clone will speak English, an English reference clip usually sounds more natural.
  • You can clone across languages, but the result may keep a light accent from the original clip.
  • Write numbers as words, or turn on Normalize numbers to words.
  • Start with defaults. Only change Advanced settings if something specific feels off.

Privacy & permission

Voice Cloner runs locally on your machine using the OmniVoice model stored in this project. Still, please only clone voices you have the right to use — your own voice, or someone who gave clear permission.

Do not use this tool to impersonate people for scams, fraud, or anything harmful.
